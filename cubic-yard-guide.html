Cubic Yard Calculator Guide

A practical walkthrough of measuring a project area and turning those measurements into a cubic yard volume calculator estimate.

  1. Measure the length

    Run a tape measure along the longest edge of your project area, in a straight line, from one boundary to the other. Record the number and the unit you measured in, such as feet or meters.

  2. Measure the width

    Measure the shorter edge, perpendicular to the length. For irregular rectangular areas, take the width at a few points and use an average if it varies noticeably.

  3. Measure the depth

    Depth is how thick the layer of material needs to be, such as 4 inches of mulch or 6 inches of gravel base. Depth is usually measured in inches even when length and width are measured in feet.

  4. Convert inches to feet

    Because the cubic yard formula works in feet, divide any inch measurement by 12 before calculating. For example, a 4 inch depth becomes 4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 ft. The calculator on this site does this conversion automatically when you select "Inches" as the unit.

  5. Calculate cubic feet

    Multiply length × width × depth, with every measurement in feet, to get the volume in cubic feet. For a circular area, calculate the area first using π × radius², then multiply by depth.

  1. Convert cubic feet to cubic yards

    Divide the cubic feet result by 27, since one cubic yard equals 27 cubic feet. This is the number most bulk material suppliers use for pricing and delivery.

  2. Calculate circular areas

    For round garden beds, tree rings, or circular excavations, measure the diameter across the widest point, then let the calculator divide it in half to get the radius and apply the circle area formula automatically.

  3. Handle irregular areas

    Break an irregular shape into smaller rectangles or circles, calculate the volume of each section separately using the calculator, and add the cubic yard results together for a combined estimate.

  4. Why actual material requirements may vary

    Ground that is uneven, sloped, or requires compaction can change how much material you actually need compared to the calculated volume. It's common practice to order a small buffer beyond the calculated amount for larger projects, and to confirm quantities with your supplier before ordering.

This guide provides general measuring practices. For projects with structural requirements, such as concrete footings or foundations, follow the specifications provided by a qualified professional.
