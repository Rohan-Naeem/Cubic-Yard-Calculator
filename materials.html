Using Cubic Yard Calculations for Bulk Materials

Volume is the common measurement behind every bulk material order. Here's how the cubic yard calculator applies to common project materials.

The cubic yard calculator on this site calculates volume, not weight. Volume is the space a material occupies, measured in cubic yards, cubic feet, or cubic meters. Once you know the volume your project requires, that same number applies regardless of which bulk material you're ordering, because volume doesn't change with material type.

Gravel

Gravel is commonly ordered by the cubic yard for driveways, walkways, and drainage projects. Measure the length and width of the area to be covered and the depth of gravel you want, then use the calculator to find your cubic yard volume before requesting a quote from a supplier.

Sand

Sand for paver bases, play areas, or leveling layers is typically ordered in a similar way — calculate the area to be covered and the desired depth, then convert to cubic yards to compare supplier pricing.

Soil

Topsoil, garden soil, or fill soil for raised beds and grading projects can be estimated the same way. Because soil settles over time, some project planners add a small buffer to the calculated volume.

Mulch

Mulch is usually spread at a shallower depth than gravel or soil, often 2 to 4 inches. Enter your bed dimensions and desired mulch depth into the calculator to get a cubic yard estimate for ordering.

Concrete

Concrete slabs, footings, and pads are calculated the same way as any rectangular volume: length × width × depth, converted to cubic yards. Concrete suppliers typically sell by the cubic yard, so this calculator's primary result lines up directly with how ready-mix concrete is quoted.

Fill

Fill material for leveling, backfilling, or building up low areas is calculated the same way as soil or gravel — determine the volume of space that needs to be filled and convert it to cubic yards.

A note on material weight

This calculator does not provide weight conversions, because the weight of one cubic yard of any material depends on that material's density, moisture content, particle size, and how compacted it is. A cubic yard of dry sand does not weigh the same as a cubic yard of wet sand or a cubic yard of gravel. For weight estimates, check with your material supplier, who can provide density figures specific to the product they sell.
